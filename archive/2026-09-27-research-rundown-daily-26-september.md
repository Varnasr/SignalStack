## FIVE THINGS WORTH KNOWING

### 1. New Gulf jobs for Nepalis have fallen by more than half

New labour approvals for Nepalis taking jobs in Gulf countries fell **52.12 per cent** between the start of the Iran conflict and mid-September compared with the same period last year, from 228,165 to 109,249. The decline was steepest for the UAE, at 59.05 per cent, followed by Saudi Arabia at 57.02 per cent. [The Kathmandu Post](https://kathmandupost.com/national/2026/09/26/gulf-job-opportunities-are-narrowing-for-nepalis?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

This is a labour-market shock that Nepal’s headline remittance numbers do not yet show. Remittance inflows rose 37.1 per cent in FY2025-26, and were still 21.2 per cent higher year-on-year by mid-August. Around 40 to 45 per cent of Nepal’s remittances come from the Gulf. [The Kathmandu Post](https://kathmandupost.com/national/2026/09/26/gulf-job-opportunities-are-narrowing-for-nepalis?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

The apparent contradiction is mostly timing. Today’s remittances are sent by people already abroad, while new labour approvals measure workers entering the employment pipeline. Renewals also make departures look steadier than new recruitment actually is.

This makes labour approvals a potentially useful **leading indicator**. If Gulf hiring remains depressed, the effects on remittances and household consumption may appear with a lag.

### 2. Maharashtra has declared 265 of its 358 talukas drought-affected

The Maharashtra government has declared **265 talukas across 32 districts** drought-affected after prolonged dry spells and a rainfall deficit. The affected talukas represent roughly 74 per cent of the state. Rainfall between 1 June and 18 September was 18.8 per cent below normal. [Akashvani News](https://newsonair.gov.in/maharashtra-declares-265-talukas-drought-affected/?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

The declaration activates measures including crop-loan restructuring, land-revenue concessions, delayed recovery of agricultural loans, electricity-bill relief for agricultural pumps, relaxed employment-guarantee rules and provision for drinking water and fodder. District collectors have also been instructed to assess crop losses using photographs and GPS coordinates. [Akashvani News](https://newsonair.gov.in/maharashtra-declares-265-talukas-drought-affected/?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

The interesting measurement issue is the relationship between rainfall and agricultural loss. An 18.8 per cent seasonal deficit does not translate mechanically into an 18.8 per cent production loss. Timing, crop stage, irrigation access, soil moisture and the length of dry spells all matter.

That makes the forthcoming crop-loss assessments considerably more important than the statewide rainfall number.

### 3. Bangladesh and Nepal are both asking the UN to postpone LDC graduation

The UN General Assembly’s Second Committee is scheduled to consider Bangladesh’s request for a **three-year postponement of graduation from least-developed-country status on 7 October**. Nepal has also requested a three-year deferral. [The Business Standard](https://www.tbsnews.net/economy/bangladeshs-ldc-graduation-when-and-how-will-un-decide-1554346?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

Bangladesh is due to graduate in November 2026. The government argues that additional time is needed because of external shocks and the economic adjustment required before preferential trade treatment is withdrawn. Bangladeshi officials say the LDC group, China and the G77 have expressed support in informal consultations, although the General Assembly will make the decision. [The Daily Star](https://www.thedailystar.net/business/economy/news/ldc-deferment-proposal-gaining-major-support-titumir-says-4283586?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

Graduation is often presented as a development milestone. Economically, it is also a change in policy treatment. Bangladesh in particular must prepare for changes in tariff preferences, pharmaceutical rules and other concessions.

The useful evaluation question is therefore counterfactual: what adjustment would three additional years permit that cannot reasonably be completed under the existing timetable?

### 4. Sri Lanka’s services exports are growing much faster than merchandise exports

Sri Lanka exported an estimated **US$12.01 billion** in goods and services during January to August, 4.26 per cent more than in the corresponding period of 2025. Merchandise exports increased 3.63 per cent to US$9.41 billion. Services exports rose 6.59 per cent to US$2.60 billion. [Sri Lanka Business Portal](https://www.srilankabusiness.com/news/sri-lankas-exports-surpass-us-12-bn-in-first-eight-months-of-2026.html?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

The August figures make the divergence clearer. Services exports increased 13.97 per cent year-on-year to US$331.87 million, while merchandise exports declined 1 per cent to US$1.27 billion. [Sri Lanka Business Portal](https://www.srilankabusiness.com/news/sri-lankas-exports-surpass-us-12-bn-in-first-eight-months-of-2026.html?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

There is a qualification. The Export Development Board describes some components as estimates, including services and parts of merchandise trade. These are therefore useful early indicators rather than final balance-of-payments data.

Still, the composition deserves attention. Sri Lanka’s post-crisis external adjustment is usually discussed through tourism and merchandise trade. ICT/BPM, transport, construction and other traded services increasingly belong in that account.

### 5. India has started rating buildings for digital connectivity

The Telecom Regulatory Authority of India launched its **Digital Connectivity Rating Platform** on 25 September. Buildings can be assessed on fibre readiness, mobile-network availability, in-building systems and Wi-Fi infrastructure, with ratings publicly searchable by consumers. [Press Information Bureau](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleaseIframePage.aspx?PRID=2314867&lang=2&reg=48&utm_source=chatgpt.com)

This is a different approach to digital infrastructure measurement. Conventional telecom statistics tell us whether a network reaches an area. They say much less about whether the signal works adequately inside the apartment, office, hospital or school where somebody tries to use it.

The platform therefore moves the unit of measurement from network coverage towards the property.

Its usefulness will depend on adoption and on whether ratings predict users’ actual experience. A technically compliant building with poor real-world service would expose the same gap between infrastructure and effective access that appeared in the 23 September Daily.

# ONE PAPER WORTH READING

### Government support and agroforestry adoption in Madhya Pradesh

Chandra Singha and colleagues study whether government support changes smallholders’ decisions to grow commercial trees. They combine survey data from **870 farm households in 59 villages in Madhya Pradesh** with geospatial information on climate, ecology and other conditions affecting tree cultivation. Programme participation is associated with a 19 per cent increase in commercial tree-growing participation, 89 per cent more commercial trees overall and 71 per cent more trees per hectare. Farmers who knew about programmes but did not participate showed no comparable increase. [Nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/s43247-026-04098-3?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

That last distinction is particularly useful. Programme awareness and programme exposure are different treatments.

The causal language deserves some care. This is observational programme evaluation rather than a randomised allocation of support. Households entering government programmes may differ systematically from non-participants in motivation, land characteristics, market access or administrative connections. Statistical adjustment cannot guarantee that those differences disappear.

Still, the paper provides useful evidence that implementation matters beyond information provision. Telling farmers that agroforestry programmes exist appears insufficient if seedlings, technical assistance, incentives or other programme components remain inaccessible.

**Citation:** Singha, C., H. Cardenas-Diaz, Z. Luo, et al. 2026. “Government support drives agroforestry adoption and tree cultivation in smallholder farming systems.” *Communications Earth & Environment*. Published 26 September 2026. # ONE NUMBER

## 52.12 per cent

The year-on-year decline in **new labour approvals for Nepalis going to Gulf countries** since the Iran conflict began. Approvals fell from 228,165 to 109,249. [The Kathmandu Post](https://kathmandupost.com/national/2026/09/26/gulf-job-opportunities-are-narrowing-for-nepalis?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

This is more informative today than Nepal’s still-strong remittance growth.

Remittances are a lagging stock-flow outcome generated largely by workers already overseas. New labour approvals tell us something about the future population of migrant workers. If the fall persists, the effect on household transfers should appear later.

It is a good example of why the indicator that moves first is not necessarily the indicator receiving the most attention.

# POLICY WATCH

## Maharashtra’s drought declaration turns a climate indicator into an entitlement decision

Declaring 265 talukas drought-affected does more than describe rainfall. It determines where households and farmers become eligible for specific forms of public assistance.

The government has announced crop-loan restructuring, land-revenue concessions, agricultural electricity relief, drinking-water and fodder provision, and relaxed rules under the employment-guarantee programme. Financial compensation for crop losses will follow further assessment. [Akashvani News](https://newsonair.gov.in/maharashtra-declares-265-talukas-drought-affected/?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

That makes the classification system important.

A rainfall threshold is observable relatively quickly. Household loss is not. Irrigated and rainfed farms within the same taluka can experience very different outcomes, while two locations with similar rainfall deficits may have different soil moisture, reservoir access and crop calendars.

The next evidence to watch is therefore the **crop-loss assessment**, including how field observations, remote sensing and administrative records are combined and how disputes over eligibility are handled.

# EDITORIAL NOTE

## Remittances can look healthy after the labour market has turned

Nepal offers an unusually clear example of a lag problem.

If recruitment falls today, the stock of workers already employed overseas continues sending money home. Some workers renew permits. Others draw on savings. Exchange-rate changes can alter the domestic value of transfers. Formalisation can move money from informal channels into recorded remittances.

The headline series can therefore continue rising after the labour market generating future remittances has weakened.

For research, this means treating migration as a sequence rather than a single variable: **job demand → recruitment → labour approval → departure → employment → earnings → remittance**.

Different datasets observe different points in that sequence.

A model using current remittance inflows as its only measure of migration exposure could consequently identify the shock late. Recruitment and labour-approval data may provide earlier information about what households will face six or twelve months later.

**Reader question:** Are there good South Asian studies that combine destination-country vacancies or recruitment data with labour permits and subsequent household remittances, allowing us to estimate the lag between a migrant-labour demand shock and its effect on household income?

That is worth returning to in Methods Monday when we look at leading, contemporaneous and lagging indicators in development data.
