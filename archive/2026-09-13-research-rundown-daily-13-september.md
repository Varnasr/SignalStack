## five things worth knowing

### 1. BRICS has put development-finance guarantees on the table

The New Delhi Declaration adopted at the BRICS summit moves the proposed BRICS Multilateral Guarantees initiative a little further towards something operational. Leaders called for technical work and pilot transactions, with the stated aim of using guarantees to improve project creditworthiness, lower financing costs and draw private capital into development projects in BRICS countries and the wider Global South. ([Prime Minister of India](https://www.pmindia.gov.in/en/news_updates/brics-new-delhi-declaration-building-for-resilience-innovation-cooperation-and-sustainability-september-12-2026/?comment=disable))

This deserves attention because guarantees can address a specific development-finance problem. Infrastructure projects in lower-income countries often face financing costs far above those in richer economies, even when the underlying project risk does not explain the entire spread.

The declaration does not tell us the guarantee structure, capital base, pricing, eligibility rules or allocation of losses. Those details will determine whether the initiative absorbs risks that private investors will not bear, merely subsidises projects that would have been financed anyway, or remains another BRICS proposal under discussion.

The first pilot transactions will be more informative than the declaration.

### 2. Pakistan has approved an US$58 billion power plan, despite serious disagreement inside the regulator

Pakistan’s National Electric Power Regulatory Authority has conditionally approved the Integrated System Plan 2025, covering generation and transmission investment through 2035. The plan implies roughly US$47.08 billion in additional generation capacity and US$10.65 billion in transmission investment. ([Dawn](https://www.dawn.com/news/amp/2029253))

The regulatory record is unusually revealing. All three NEPRA members wrote dissenting or separate advisory observations. Among their concerns were changes to project selection and whether the Council of Common Interests had been bypassed. The reference scenario assumes average GDP growth of 3.52 per cent and would add 26,045 MW while retiring 2,577 MW, leaving installed capacity at 62,657 MW. ([Dawn](https://www.dawn.com/news/amp/2029253))

The number therefore needs to be read as a planning envelope rather than committed expenditure.

There is a useful research question here. Electricity plans depend heavily on demand forecasts, fuel assumptions and the treatment of distributed generation. Pakistan is making investment choices lasting decades while each of those variables is changing quickly.

### 3. India’s food-warning-label dispute now has a deadline

The Supreme Court has ordered the Union government to provide a clear implementation pathway for front-of-pack warning labels within ten days. The order followed concern that FSSAI’s proposed two-stage system could leave the stricter second stage indefinitely delayed. ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/legal/litigation/india-court-orders-regulator-provide-timeline-food-warning-labels-2026-09-11/))

This is a substantive development from the labelling debate covered earlier in Research Rundown. FSSAI had proposed initially requiring red warning symbols when products exceeded limits in at least two of three categories: added sugar, salt and saturated fat. A stricter second phase would follow. The Court said uncertainty over the timing of that second phase could not be justified by industry reformulation needs or assessments of consumer acceptance. A government source told Reuters that FSSAI was willing to consider implementing both phases together. ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/legal/litigation/india-court-orders-regulator-provide-timeline-food-warning-labels-2026-09-11/))

The next document matters more than another round of statements. Thresholds, reference quantities, implementation dates and exemptions will determine which products actually receive warnings.

### 4. Nepal’s flood damage is now drawing down the electricity system’s winter reserve

The 26 August Bhotekoshi floods knocked more than 400 MW of hydropower capacity offline. Nepal Electricity Authority has consequently been running all three stages of the reservoir-based Kulekhani system continuously during the monsoon. Kulekhani is normally conserved for the dry winter months, when run-of-river generation falls. ([Kathmandu Post](https://kathmandupost.com/national/2026/09/12/bhotekoshi-floods-push-kulekhani-into-continuous-power-generation?utm_source=chatgpt.com))

This gives the flood a second-order cost that is easy to miss in damage assessments. Lost generation is one cost. Using stored water now reduces the buffer available against a later seasonal shortage.

The episode also illustrates why infrastructure resilience cannot be measured asset by asset. Damage to one group of hydropower plants changes how another part of the electricity system must operate.

Nepal’s reconstruction assessment should therefore be read alongside winter generation adequacy, reservoir levels and electricity-import requirements.

### 5. Bangladesh has begun a three-month national dengue campaign as the peak season approaches

Bangladesh launched its Dengue Prevention and Cleanliness Campaign 2026 on 12 September. The three-month programme is being run jointly by the Ministry of Local Government, Rural Development and Co-operatives and the two Dhaka city corporations. ([The Business Standard](https://www.tbsnews.net/bangladesh/pm-opens-three-month-nationwide-dengue-prevention-cleanliness-campaign-1540281?utm_source=chatgpt.com))

The campaign itself establishes little about whether transmission will fall. Dengue control is particularly susceptible to activity measures that look impressive administratively: inspections conducted, sites cleaned, awareness events held.

The useful indicators are epidemiological and operational. Case incidence, spatial concentration, entomological surveillance and the time between identifying breeding sites and removing them would tell us considerably more about whether the intervention changes exposure.

Bangladesh’s campaign is therefore worth following through its data rather than its launch.

## one paper worth reading

### Phone tutoring works during school closures. SMS alone is much less reliable.

Noam Angrist and colleagues report randomized trials of remote primary-school instruction in India, Nepal, Kenya, Uganda and the Philippines. Children received targeted tutoring by phone plus SMS, SMS alone, or usual provision. The combined phone intervention increased the pooled learning outcome by 0.321 standard deviations across the five countries; SMS alone produced a much smaller 0.078 SD effect and worked inconsistently across settings. India contributed 708 participants and Nepal 2,678. ([Nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-026-10990-x?utm_source=chatgpt.com))

The cross-country design is the paper’s main strength. Random assignment supports causal inference within the trials, and Nepal and the Philippines also randomized delivery between government and NGO instructors. Government delivery retained substantial effects. ([Nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-026-10990-x?utm_source=chatgpt.com))

There is an important qualification. Effect sizes grew across successive trials as targeting and implementation improved. Context and intervention maturity therefore move together, making some cross-country comparisons difficult to interpret. These were also emergency interventions during COVID-era school disruption, when the counterfactual in some countries involved very little schooling.

The policy result is still useful: low-tech, targeted human instruction travelled across settings far better than messages alone. For education systems planning for climate, conflict or disease-related closures, that is actionable evidence.

**Citation:** Angrist, Noam, Micheal Ainomugisha, Sai Pramod Bathena, et al. 2026. “Mobile education builds resilience during shocks in five countries.” *Nature*. Published 9 September 2026. ## one number

### 0.321 SD

The pooled effect of targeted phone tutoring plus SMS on the study’s average numeracy outcome across randomized trials in India, Nepal, Kenya, Uganda and the Philippines. SMS alone produced an estimated effect of 0.078 SD. ([Nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-026-10990-x?utm_source=chatgpt.com))

That difference is more interesting than the headline effect size.

Both interventions used ordinary mobile phones. The larger effect came from adding a person who could assess a child’s level and adjust instruction accordingly.

Technology access was therefore only one part of the treatment.

## policy watch

### BRICS has produced a long list of institutions. Now watch which ones acquire budgets and rules.

The New Delhi Declaration creates or advances an unusual number of mechanisms with development-policy relevance.

BRICS members agreed to establish AGRIN, an agro-inputs, genetic-resources and information network, alongside networks on agroecology, regenerative agriculture and digital agriculture. They also backed further work on a BRICS grain-trading platform. ([Prime Minister of India](https://www.pmindia.gov.in/en/news_updates/brics-new-delhi-declaration-building-for-resilience-innovation-cooperation-and-sustainability-september-12-2026/?comment=disable))

In health, the declaration establishes a network of mental-health Centres of Excellence coordinated by NIMHANS and backs a BRICS Mission for Healthy Lifestyle for 2026–29. It also proposes greater cooperation between medical-product regulators and an integrated early-warning system for infectious diseases. ([Prime Minister of India](https://www.pmindia.gov.in/en/news_updates/brics-new-delhi-declaration-building-for-resilience-innovation-cooperation-and-sustainability-september-12-2026/?comment=disable))

Finance ministers and central banks will continue work on cross-border payment interoperability and settlement using national currencies. The declaration carefully describes this as continuing technical work and explicitly recognises different national regulatory arrangements. ([Prime Minister of India](https://www.pmindia.gov.in/en/news_updates/brics-new-delhi-declaration-building-for-resilience-innovation-cooperation-and-sustainability-september-12-2026/?comment=disable))

On climate, the political position is more explicit. BRICS opposes carbon-border adjustment measures it considers unilateral or protectionist, while acknowledging a continuing role for fossil fuels in emerging economies. It simultaneously backs the Paris Agreement, adaptation finance, carbon-market cooperation and community-based adaptation. ([Prime Minister of India](https://www.pmindia.gov.in/en/news_updates/brics-new-delhi-declaration-building-for-resilience-innovation-cooperation-and-sustainability-september-12-2026/?comment=disable))

These commitments should be separated into three categories as implementation proceeds: institutions that acquire money and operating rules, technical networks that produce usable public goods, and declarations that remain statements of common position.

The distinction will make the next year of BRICS activity much easier to assess.

## editorial note

### Count institutions when they start doing something

International declarations produce an awkward measurement problem.

The New Delhi document contains networks, centres, platforms, working groups, initiatives, principles, roadmaps and proposed financing mechanisms. Counting them would produce a substantial indicator of international cooperation.

It would tell us very little about results.

A useful monitoring framework would start one step later. Does an initiative acquire a secretariat or accountable lead institution? Is there a budget? Are participation and eligibility rules public? Does it produce transactions, datasets, regulatory agreements or services that did not previously exist?

The proposed Multilateral Guarantees initiative provides a particularly clean test. The declaration now calls for pilot transactions. ([Prime Minister of India](https://www.pmindia.gov.in/en/news_updates/brics-new-delhi-declaration-building-for-resilience-innovation-cooperation-and-sustainability-september-12-2026/?comment=disable)) Once those appear, we can ask which projects receive guarantees, how risk is priced, how much private finance follows, and whether borrowing costs actually fall.

Until then, the correct unit is a proposal.

**Reader question:** If you were building a scorecard to distinguish functioning international cooperation from summit announcements, which two or three observable indicators would you require before counting an initiative as operational?

This would make a good future Methods Monday: how to measure institutional implementation when governments report activities more readily than outputs.
