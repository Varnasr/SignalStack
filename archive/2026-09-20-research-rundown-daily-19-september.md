## FIVE THINGS WORTH KNOWING

### 1. India’s climate extremes are becoming more geographically differentiated

A 52-year analysis of daily rainfall and temperature data finds a statistically significant increase in the area of India exposed to climate extremes. The revised India Climate Extremes Index rises by about **0.36 per cent of area per decade** between 1971 and 2022. ([Royal Meteorological Society (RMetS)](https://rmets.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/joc.70467?utm_source=chatgpt.com))

The national trend conceals different regional changes. Northwestern India shows the strongest increases in maximum-temperature extremes, heat index and heatwave severity. South Peninsular India has seen increasing drought-related extremes. Northeast India is unusual: intense one-day rainfall is increasing alongside longer dry spells and drought episodes. ([Royal Meteorological Society (RMetS)](https://rmets.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/joc.70467?utm_source=chatgpt.com))

That last result matters for adaptation. A district facing heavier downpours and longer dry periods needs water storage, drainage and agricultural planning designed around greater variability. A simple classification as increasingly “wet” or “dry” misses the problem.

There is a measurement qualification. The researchers combine seven indicators into a revised composite index. Composite indices are useful for tracking several hazards together, but movements in the headline score depend partly on indicator choice and aggregation. The component measures remain important.

### 3. Bangladesh has gazetted a large public-sector pay increase

Bangladesh has issued its ninth national pay scale, effective retrospectively from 1 July 2026. Basic salaries rise by as much as **142 per cent**, with the increase being introduced in phases. The existing special allowance will be withdrawn and adjusted against arrears. ([The Business Standard](https://www.tbsnews.net/bangladesh/gazette-issued-national-pay-scale-2026-1547116?utm_source=chatgpt.com))

The phasing matters. Employees in Grade 9 and above initially receive 40 per cent of their additional basic pay, while Grades 10 to 20 receive 50 per cent. Higher proportions apply from January 2027 before full implementation. ([The Business Standard](https://www.tbsnews.net/bangladesh/gazette-issued-national-pay-scale-2026-1547116?utm_source=chatgpt.com))

This is a substantial fiscal and labour-market intervention. The questions now concern the aggregate wage bill, financing and spillovers into private-sector wage demands.

Nominal increases also need to be separated from real gains. Bangladesh has experienced sustained inflation since the previous pay structure was introduced. Comparing the old and new salary tables alone will overstate the improvement in purchasing power.

### 4. Bangladesh’s garment unions want the minimum wage reviewed under the new three-year rule

The IndustriALL Bangladesh Council has called for a new garment-sector wage board by 30 September and a revised minimum wage by the end of November. The current minimum wage dates from November 2023. ([The Business Standard](https://www.tbsnews.net/economy/rmg/industriall-demands-wage-board-reconstitution-new-minimum-wage-garment-workers-1547676?utm_source=chatgpt.com))

The timing follows an amendment to the Bangladesh Labour Act that shortened the statutory minimum-wage review period from five years to three. The present wage therefore reaches its new review point this November. ([The Business Standard](https://www.tbsnews.net/economy/rmg/industriall-demands-wage-board-reconstitution-new-minimum-wage-garment-workers-1547676?utm_source=chatgpt.com))

This deserves attention beyond the wage number eventually announced. Bangladesh’s garment industry employs millions of workers and remains central to export earnings. The relevant comparison is between wage growth, inflation and productivity since 2023.

Compliance matters too. A statutory wage is an institutional floor. Earnings depend on enforcement, hours, overtime payments, employment continuity and whether factories respond by changing workforce composition or hours.

## ONE PAPER WORTH READING

### Evolution of Climate Extremes Over the Indian Subcontinent Using a Revised CEI

Thota Mohan and colleagues use IMD gridded daily rainfall and temperature data from **1971 to 2022** to construct a revised India Climate Extremes Index. Seven components capture maximum and minimum temperature, consecutive dry days, heavy precipitation, drought using SPEI, humid heat and heatwave severity. Trend estimation shows widespread increases in heat extremes, particularly in northwestern India; stronger drought signals in southern India; and the unusual combination of more short-duration heavy rainfall and increasing drought episodes in the Northeast. At the national level, the index increases significantly by roughly 0.36 per cent of area per decade. ([Royal Meteorological Society (RMetS)](https://rmets.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/joc.70467?utm_source=chatgpt.com))

The regional decomposition is more useful than the composite headline. Combining different hazards into one index necessarily embeds decisions about indicators and aggregation, while regional averages can still conceal local exposure. The observational record also identifies trends rather than attributing individual changes to specific causal mechanisms.

Its contribution is practical. Indian climate adaptation cannot sensibly be organised around one national hazard trajectory. Heat, drought and rainfall variability are changing differently across regions.

## POLICY WATCH

### Bangladesh is about to run two wage experiments at once

Bangladesh’s new public-sector pay scale and the impending garment-sector minimum-wage review create an unusual opportunity to observe two different wage-setting institutions during the same inflationary period.

Public salaries are administratively determined and financed through the budget. Garment wages emerge from a statutory wage-board process inside a highly trade-exposed private industry.

The outcomes should therefore be compared carefully.

A useful assessment would track real wages rather than announced nominal increases, and examine employment, hours, overtime and worker turnover alongside monthly earnings. For the public sector, the corresponding questions concern the wage bill, recruitment and fiscal displacement.

There is a broader distributional question. Large formal-sector wage adjustments can restore purchasing power for covered workers while leaving informal workers facing the same price level without an equivalent adjustment mechanism.

## EDITORIAL NOTE

### A national climate average is becoming a poor description of the risk

The climate-extremes paper is useful because it makes a problem with adaptation planning unusually visible.

Northeast India can experience longer dry spells while extreme one-day rainfall becomes more frequent. There is no contradiction. Seasonal rainfall totals, number of rainy days, rainfall intensity and the length of dry intervals measure different properties of the monsoon.

Policy often compresses them.

Agricultural planning may use seasonal rainfall. Urban drainage depends heavily on rainfall intensity. Reservoir management depends on timing and runoff. Household water security can deteriorate when rain arrives in fewer, heavier episodes even if the annual total changes little.

The methodological implication is straightforward. “Rainfall” is rarely an adequate exposure variable by itself.

For development research, the choice of climate measure should follow the outcome being studied. A crop-yield model, diarrhoeal-disease study and urban-flood analysis may require quite different representations of the same monsoon.

**Reader question:** Which climate-exposure measures have proved most useful in South Asian household or agricultural panel data when seasonal rainfall totals conceal changes in dry-spell duration and extreme rainfall intensity?

This is worth carrying into a future Methods Monday on matching climate exposure measures to development outcomes.
