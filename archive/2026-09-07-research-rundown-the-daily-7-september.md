## Five things worth knowing

### Nepal’s glacial-lake programme faces a design problem

Nepal plans to spend roughly US$50 million lowering four high-risk glacial lakes in the Koshi and Gandaki basins, with US$36.16 million coming from the Green Climate Fund. The programme could cover mountain areas containing an estimated 2.3 million people, but scientists interviewed by the *Kathmandu Post* argue that modest lake lowering, modelled on earlier three-metre interventions, may leave substantial downstream risk intact. ([Kathmandu Post](https://kathmandupost.com/climate-environment/2026/09/06/nepal-is-draining-four-glacial-lakes-scientists-say-it-is-not-enough))

The criticism is specific. Researchers want lake-by-lake hydrological modelling, stronger outlet management, early-warning systems and tighter controls on infrastructure construction within likely flood paths. Nepal has more than 2,000 glacial lakes, while a 2020 ICIMOD-UNDP assessment identified 21 in Nepal as potentially dangerous. ([Kathmandu Post](https://kathmandupost.com/climate-environment/2026/09/06/nepal-is-draining-four-glacial-lakes-scientists-say-it-is-not-enough))

Why it matters: after the 26 August disaster, adaptation spending will rise. The harder question is whether projects are designed around measured risk reduction or around interventions that fit available budgets.

### Bangladesh clears most of the trade barriers identified by the EU

Bangladesh says it has resolved 48 of 61 non-tariff barriers and procedural bottlenecks identified by the European Union since March. Changes include easing licence renewals for wholly foreign-owned logistics firms, doubling the annual ceiling for commercial-sample imports from US$10,000 to US$20,000, and changing customs treatment of traceability smart cards. ([The Business Standard](https://www.tbsnews.net/economy/48-eu-non-tariff-barriers-cleared-13-more-being-resolved-commerce-minister-1534806?utm_source=chatgpt.com))

Dhaka is preparing for talks on an EU free-trade agreement and investment-protection agreement while separately seeking a three-year delay to its scheduled 24 November 2026 graduation from least-developed-country status. An FTA has not yet been negotiated, and the government’s count of “resolved” barriers is its own assessment. ([BSS](https://www.bssnews.net/news/421582?utm_source=chatgpt.com))

Why it matters: the EU is Bangladesh’s largest export market. The regulatory changes offer an early indication of how Bangladesh is preparing for a trading regime in which preferential LDC access can no longer be assumed indefinitely.

### India’s biodiversity benefit-sharing system passes ₹200 crore

India’s National Biodiversity Authority says cumulative disbursements under the Access and Benefit-Sharing system have crossed ₹200 crore. Its latest allocation sends ₹8.23 crore to state and Union Territory biodiversity bodies from commercial use of bitter gourd, okra, chilli and onion genetic resources. ([Akashvani News](https://newsonair.gov.in/nba-achieves-milestone-with-over-%E2%82%B9200-crore-abs-disbursements/?utm_source=chatgpt.com))

There is an interesting allocation problem inside the announcement. Because the resources were obtained through markets or traders and individual providers could not be identified, proceeds are being distributed according to states’ shares of cultivation rather than to identifiable source communities. ([Akashvani News](https://newsonair.gov.in/nba-achieves-milestone-with-over-%E2%82%B9200-crore-abs-disbursements/?utm_source=chatgpt.com))

Why it matters: benefit sharing sounds straightforward until biological resources move through commercial supply chains. The allocation rule determines who receives compensation when provenance is uncertain.

### Pakistan returns to international bond markets with a US$3 billion issue

Pakistan issued a US$3 billion dual-tranche Eurobond last week, its largest single international bond transaction to date, according to Finance Minister Muhammad Aurangzeb. The government says its tax-to-GDP ratio has risen from 8.1 to 10.3 per cent and points to three consecutive primary surpluses as evidence of improved fiscal management. ([Radio Pakistan](https://radio.gov.pk/03-09-2026/pakistan-issues-three-billion-dollar-bond))

A successful bond sale demonstrates access to external financing. It says much less about whether Pakistan has resolved the fiscal structure that repeatedly produces balance-of-payments pressure. Borrowing costs, maturity and the use of the proceeds matter for that assessment.

Why it matters: Pakistan is trying to replace expensive short-term borrowing and reduce rollover risk. Whether the new debt improves the maturity structure will matter more than the headline size of the issue.

## One paper worth reading

### South Asia expanded education faster than it expanded economic opportunity

Maurizio Bussolo, Vito Peragine and Fabian Reutzel ask whether circumstances outside an individual’s control have become less predictive of education, labour-market outcomes and consumption across South Asia. Their harmonised dataset contains about 19 million observations from seven countries. Using repeated cross-sections to construct pseudo-panels, they compare cohorts born from the 1950s through the 1990s. ([OUP Academic](https://academic.oup.com/ooec/advance-article/doi/10.1093/ooec/odag007/8767246))

Educational inequality of opportunity falls substantially across cohorts. Consumption inequality of opportunity remains high. The authors identify two important links between those results: much of the educational expansion occurred at basic-schooling levels with relatively low labour-market returns, while women’s educational gains have not been matched by comparable increases in labour-force participation. Circumstances also continue to predict labour outcomes among people with similar education. ([OUP Academic](https://academic.oup.com/ooec/advance-article/doi/10.1093/ooec/odag007/8767246))

The caution concerns interpretation. Inequality-of-opportunity estimates depend on which predetermined circumstances surveys observe, and pseudo-panels reconstruct cohort trajectories rather than following the same people through time. Unobserved circumstances will generally leave measured inequality below the underlying concept.

The paper still makes a valuable regional contribution. Expanding schooling can equalise one important input without equalising the institutions through which education becomes employment and consumption.

Bussolo, Maurizio, Vito Peragine and Fabian Reutzel. 2026. “Inequality of Opportunity in South Asia: The Puzzle of Educational Gains Without Consumption Gains.” *Oxford Open Economics*, odag007. Published 20 August 2026. DOI: 10.1093/ooec/odag007. ([OUP Academic](https://academic.oup.com/ooec/advance-article/doi/10.1093/ooec/odag007/8767246))

## One number

### 175 households

That is the sample behind a new IIED report estimating the health and economic effects of extreme heat on informal workers in Dhaka. Researchers used the Comprehensive Climate Impact Quantification toolkit to examine losses experienced at work and at home. ([IIED](https://www.iied.org/22755iied))

The number is worth foregrounding because some coverage of heat-related productivity losses now produces impressively precise monetary estimates. A survey of 175 households can document mechanisms and experiences in considerable detail. It cannot, without strong modelling assumptions, establish a population-wide estimate for Dhaka’s informal workforce.

The report is still useful. Heat-related illness, lost working time and household costs are poorly captured in conventional economic accounts. The appropriate response is to read the estimates with their sampling and modelling assumptions attached.

Bharadwaj, Ritu, N. Karthikeyan, Swati Chaliha, Shakirul Islam and Urmi Jahan Tanni. 2026. *No Escaping the Heat: Health and Economic Impacts of Heat Stress on Informal Workers in Urban Bangladesh*. IIED.

### India sets out a new maternal and newborn health roadmap to 2030

The Union Health Ministry has introduced the SUMAN Roadmap 2030, concentrating additional attention on 13 high-focus states and 130 districts while retaining a national strategy for all states and Union Territories. The ministry also issued guidelines for birth waiting homes, which place women from remote areas near health facilities before delivery. ([Press Information Bureau](https://www.pib.gov.in/newsite/pmreleases.aspx?lang=2&mincode=31.&reg=48&utm_source=chatgpt.com))

The roadmap addresses high-risk pregnancy, postpartum haemorrhage, caesarean-section use, digital health and grievance systems. Climate effects on maternal health also appear explicitly in the implementation discussions. ([Public and Policy](https://publicandpolicy.com/2026/09/03/union-ministry-of-health-and-family-welfare-convenes-national-workshop-on-suman-roadmap-2030-accelerating-the-continuum-of-care/?utm_source=chatgpt.com))

The district focus is sensible only if it changes resource allocation and service performance. India already has several maternal-health programmes, including Janani Suraksha Yojana, JSSK, LaQshya and PMSMA. Another framework adds value where it identifies persistent mortality clusters, fixes referral and quality failures, and makes responsibility measurable. The implementation documents and district-level targets will therefore matter more than the launch.

## editorial note

### Educational convergence can coexist with durable inequality

The Bussolo, Peragine and Reutzel paper is a useful corrective to one of development policy’s most persistent shortcuts: treating education as though it were a sufficient proxy for opportunity.

South Asia has changed enormously across the cohorts in their data. Schooling expanded and gender gaps in educational attainment narrowed. Yet circumstances inherited at birth remain strongly associated with consumption and labour-market outcomes. ([OUP Academic](https://academic.oup.com/ooec/advance-article/doi/10.1093/ooec/odag007/8767246))

That divergence changes the diagnostic problem. If educational inequality falls while economic inequality of opportunity persists, another year of schooling cannot carry the entire explanatory burden. Labour demand, occupational segregation, unpaid care, mobility restrictions, social networks, caste and class sorting, job quality and discrimination become part of the empirical question.

There is also a measurement warning. Years of schooling are comparatively easy to harmonise across surveys and decades. Opportunity is not. A cleaner education variable can therefore give the appearance of social convergence while harder-to-measure barriers remain in place.
