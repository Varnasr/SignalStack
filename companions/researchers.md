## Farzana Afridi

Afridi’s work covers gender, labour markets and human capital in India. Her research on women’s employment is especially useful because it connects household constraints with the structure of labour demand.

## Arjun Appadurai

Appadurai’s 2006 essay “The Right to Research” remains worth reading almost twenty years later.

His argument is that research should be understood as a general capacity for disciplined inquiry rather than an activity belonging exclusively to professional researchers. The paper draws on work with PUKAR in Mumbai and connects research capacity to democratic citizenship.

The formulation is useful. It should also make professional researchers uncomfortable in productive ways.

[https://doi.org/10.1080/14767720600750696](https://doi.org/10.1080/14767720600750696)

[https://docs.ufpr.br/~clarissa/pdfs/Research_AppaduraiA.pdf](https://docs.ufpr.br/~clarissa/pdfs/Research_AppaduraiA.pdf)

## Navroz K. Dubash

Navroz Dubash’s work on climate governance, energy policy and the Indian regulatory state is useful because it keeps climate politics attached to institutions.

His edited volume *India in a Warming World* remains a good entry point into the history of Indian climate policy and the relationship between climate action and development.

I would read Dubash particularly when discussions of India’s energy transition become a list of renewable-capacity targets. Transitions occur through electricity regulation, fiscal choices, state governments, distribution companies, coal regions, firms and political bargaining. Technology is one part of that story.

Centre for Policy Research profile and publications:

[https://cprindia.org/people/navroz-k-dubash/](https://cprindia.org/people/navroz-k-dubash/)

- From [The Research Rundown | April 2025](../archive/2025-04-28-the-research-rundown-april-2025-edition.md), 2025-04-28.

## Richa Govil

Richa Govil is Vice Chancellor of Azim Premji University’s Bengaluru campus and works on agricultural livelihoods, women in agriculture, household nutrition, farmer producer organisations and social enterprises.

Her work with Annapurna Neti on Farmer Producer Companies is especially useful because the FPO debate is often dominated by programme counts. Their research asks harder questions about capitalisation, inclusion, viability and the institutional support producer collectives require.

## Reetika Khera

*IIT Delhi.*

[Faculty page, IIT Delhi](https://hss.iitd.ac.in/faculty/reetika-khera)

Khera’s work on welfare state delivery, the public distribution system, and the political economy of social protection in India is foundational for anyone working on who actually receives state support — and who does not. Her research has directly shaped policy debates on NREGA, the PDS, and Aadhaar. As the gig economy debate intensifies and Budget 2026-27 reveals the limits of recognition without redistribution, her body of work becomes more, not less, relevant.

- From [The Research Rundown — March 2026 Edition](../archive/2026-03-18-the-research-rundown-march-2026-edition.md), 2026-03-18.

## Anjal Prakash

Anjal Prakash works on climate adaptation, water, urban resilience and gender in South Asia at the Bharti Institute of Public Policy, Indian School of Business.

A December 2024 *PLOS Climate* paper of his asks a question that adaptation programmes still handle poorly: have gendered dynamics been adequately incorporated into South Asian adaptation research and practice?

[https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pclm.0000475](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pclm.0000475)

In January 2025 he was also selected as a lead author for the IPCC Special Report on Climate Change and Cities.

[https://www.isb.edu/news/isb-in-the-news/isb-prof-to-be-lead-author-for-special-un-climate-report](https://www.isb.edu/news/isb-in-the-news/isb-prof-to-be-lead-author-for-special-un-climate-report)

Worth following if you work on climate resilience and find yourself suspicious of adaptation frameworks in which social differentiation appears in the final paragraph.

- From [The Research Rundown | April 2025](../archive/2025-04-28-the-research-rundown-april-2025-edition.md), 2025-04-28.

## Nitya Rao

*University of East Anglia.*

[Google Scholar](https://scholar.google.com/citations?user=4DJwX0AAAAAJ&hl=en) | [UEA Research Portal](https://research-portal.uea.ac.uk/en/persons/nitya-rao/)

Lead author on the PLOS Climate paper featured above. Three decades of field research on gender, agriculture, and climate change across South Asia, with a consistent intersectional lens covering class, caste, and geography. Her work bridges grassroots fieldwork and policy-level intervention in a genuinely rare way. If you work in climate adaptation or food systems in the region, follow her closely.

## Kalpana Sharma

Kalpana Sharma has spent decades writing about cities, development, gender and environment.

*Rediscovering Dharavi* remains particularly useful because it treats Dharavi as an economy and social world rather than reducing it to a visual shorthand for urban poverty.
